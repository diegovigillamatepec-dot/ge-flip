Where the prices come from

Live Grand Exchange trades from the OSRS Wiki real-time API, which has used Jagex data covering every game client since 30 September 2026. Prices refresh every minute. You place every offer yourself; nothing here touches the game.

How the live margin is worked out

  • Buy is the latest instant-sell price. A buy offer around it gets filled by people selling fast.
  • Sell is the latest instant-buy price. A sell offer around it gets filled by people buying fast.
  • Margin is Sell minus Buy minus the GE tax: 2% of the sell price, rounded down and capped at 5M per item. Items under 50 gp and the Wiki's exempt list pay nothing.
  • A warning icon marks a one-off trade far from the last hour's average. The margin uses the average instead, so a fat-finger trade can't top the list.
  • Profit / 4h is the margin times what you can move in one buy-limit window: the buy limit, or your share of the 4-hour volume if that's lower.

Are OSRS daily cycles real?

For some items, yes. A test on the 86 most-traded items over the past week (Wiki prices, November 2026) found:

  • Most items don't repeat the same shape each day. The average day matched the rest of the week with a score of only 0.20 out of 1, and only 7 of 86 items scored 0.5 or more.
  • The typical daily swing was 1.6%, smaller than the 2% GE tax. Many swings are just noise.
  • Buying near the weekly floor and waiting had no edge: it lost about 1.2% per trade after tax, the same as buying at a random time.
  • Buying at an item's usual low hour and selling at its usual high hour, only when the pattern had held on earlier days and the swing beat the tax, made about +1.3% per trade and won 62% of the time. Each trade was picked using only earlier days, so the test couldn't peek ahead.
  • Only a handful of items pass at any time (about 4 in 50 in the test), so each scan now checks the 150 most relevant items.

So the signals now follow each item's own daily pattern and skip items that don't have one. One week is a small sample, so treat the numbers as a guide.

What about leaving offers up for days?

GE offers never expire, so a second test placed a buy offer at a low the item reached on most days of the past week and a sell offer at a high it reached on most days, then replayed that on the last 30 days for the 150 most-traded items:

  • Round trips that completed made +2% to +5% each, but took 2 to 6 days.
  • About two thirds of items ended with the buy filled and the price never coming back, because a low offer fills exactly when the price is falling. Overall the method broke even, slightly below simply holding.
  • Items whose offers worked in the first half of the month roughly broke even in the second half, while the rest lost about 1.5% to 1.9%. Past results helped avoid losers more than they found big winners.

So "Set offers" only appears for items whose own 30-day replay made money, and every item shows its offer prices and replay result in its detail panel.

How a scan reads each item

  • Daily pattern: the last 7 days of hourly prices, grouped by hour of your day. Each day is compared with the others; a match score of 0.4 or more means the shape repeats.
  • Buy at / Sell at: for items with a dependable pattern, today's price level times the usual low at the cheap hour and the usual high at the peak hour. For other items, the prices reached in half of the last 24 hours.
  • Target net: Sell at minus tax minus Buy at.
  • Past-week test: the same rule replayed on each past day using only the days before it. If those trades lost money on average, or fewer than half won, the item doesn't get a buy signal.
  • Floor: the lowest price the item held for about 3 hours in the last 7 days. It's shown for context and no longer triggers a buy.

What each signal means

  • Buy now Dependable daily pattern, and it's at or near its usual low hour or already at the buy price.
  • Dip ahead Dependable pattern, but the low hour is later. The card says when.
  • Set offers No daily dip to time, but leaving a buy offer and a sell offer at the listed prices made money in a replay of the last 30 days.
  • Sell At its usual high hour, or the price just spiked. A good moment to list what you hold.
  • Dump The last 15 minutes traded well under the 24h median on heavy sell volume. Often a buy, but check the news first.
  • Falling Down more than 6% over the week, so each day's dip is lower than the last.
  • No cycle No dependable daily pattern, or one too small to beat the tax. Only flip it on a positive live margin. Thin Too few trades to tell.

Things to keep in mind

  • Buy limits reset 4 hours after your first purchase of that item.
  • Game updates, usually on Wednesdays, and new content break normal cycles.
  • Requests are paced and cached: one bulk price call per refresh, plus history only for the items a scan covers.
